\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Learned mesh simulators converged on a single objective}
\label{sec:intro:lineage}

Learned simulators for continuum mechanics descend from one architectural
idea: represent the discretised state as a graph and learn its one-step
dynamics by message passing. Graph Network-based Simulators established the
template on particle systems, predicting per-particle accelerations under an
encode--process--decode graph network and showing that a model supervised on
single timesteps generalises to longer horizons, new initial conditions and an
order of magnitude more particles at test
time~\cite{sanchezgonzalez2020gns}. MeshGraphNets carried the template onto
simulation meshes, adding a world-space edge set for contact and a learned
sizing field for adaptive remeshing, and covering cloth, structural mechanics,
incompressible flow and compressible aerodynamics at one to two orders of
magnitude below solver cost~\cite{pfaff2021meshgraphnets}.

It is worth stating plainly what these two papers optimise, because the
subsequent literature inherits it without amendment: the objective is an $L^2$
loss between the predicted per-node output and the corresponding ground-truth
value, and the reported figure of merit is an RMSE averaged over coordinates,
nodes, rollout steps and test trajectories. Neither paper emits a probabilistic
output. The stochasticity that does appear---Gaussian noise injected into
training inputs---is a regulariser that makes deterministic rollouts robust over
hundreds of steps, not a model of a predictive distribution.

The hierarchical branch grew from a purely computational diagnosis. Flat
message passing propagates information one hop per layer, so the number of
steps required scales with mesh diameter, and stacking them induces
over-smoothing and prohibitive memory cost. MultiScale MeshGraphNets learned
surrogate dynamics on a coarser mesh and passed messages at two resolutions
simultaneously~\cite{fortunato2022msmgn}; Bi-Stride Multi-Scale GNN made the
coarsening principled and automatic, pooling on every other frontier of a
breadth-first search so that no coarse mesh need be drawn by hand and
connectivity is provably preserved~\cite{cao2023bsmsgnn}, reusing the
pool/unpool-with-skip primitive of graph U-Nets~\cite{gao2019graphunets}.
X-MeshGraphNet removed the remaining partitioning
bottleneck~\cite{nabian2024xmeshgraphnet}, HOOD replaced explicit supervision
with an incremental potential~\cite{grigorev2023hood}, EAGLE and MAgNet
attacked long-range transport and mesh-free
querying~\cite{janny2023eagle, boussif2022magnet}, and Erwin brought
ball-tree attention to large point systems~\cite{zhdanov2025erwin}. Replacing
message passing altogether---by physics-attention slicing in
Transolver~\cite{wu2024transolver}, by general operator transformers in
GNOT~\cite{hao2023gnot}, by graph--Fourier hybrids in
GINO~\cite{li2023gino}, or by universal physics
transformers~\cite{alkin2024upt, alkin2025abupt}---reaches the same evaluation
endpoint by a different architectural route. Across the whole lineage,
including the operator-learning ancestors~\cite{lu2021deeponet, li2021fourier},
the reported metric is a relative $L^2$ or RMSE against \emph{one} reference
trajectory per condition.

Crucially, in every one of these papers hierarchy is justified on
receptive-field and cost grounds alone. No work in this line argues, or tests,
that coarsening changes \emph{what object} the network estimates.

\subsection{What a squared loss elicits}
\label{sec:intro:elicitation}

Decision theory says exactly what such an objective yields. Under a squared
loss the Bayes-optimal predictor is the conditional mean; under an absolute
loss, the conditional median~\cite{gneiting2007strictly}. When the conditional
law $p(u \mid c)$ of the field given the model's observable inputs is narrow,
the mean is an adequate summary and the practice is harmless. When it is
broad, the conditional mean is not a physically realisable state at all: it is
a blurred average over mutually incompatible outcomes. This is not an
optimisation failure to be engineered away---it is the \emph{optimum} of the
stated objective.

The claim is made most forcefully by GenCFD, which states in its abstract that
ensembles of deterministic models trained to minimise mean-square errors
regress to the mean flow, and supplies solvable toy models explaining why a
score-based generative alternative does not~\cite{molinaro2024gencfd}. The
vocabulary for the failure mode, however, was invented in stochastic video
prediction, where a pixel loss was diagnosed as producing a blurry average of
futures~\cite{babaeizadeh2018sv2p, denton2018svglp, lee2018savp}, and
Lucas et al.\ settled analytically that latent collapse is a property of the
Gaussian likelihood rather than a defect of the variational
bound~\cite{lucas2019dontblame}. Microstructure makes the point undeniable:
averaging grain fields over Monte Carlo seeds yields an object with no grain
boundaries at all~\cite{torquato2002random, plimpton2009spparks}.

\subsection{Engineering meshes are broad-conditional for a different reason
than weather is}
\label{sec:intro:mechanisms}

It matters a great deal \emph{why} a conditional is broad, because the three
mechanisms in play do not respond to the same modelling choice and are not
scored with the same metric. Conflating them is the largest conceptual hazard
in this area, and it is worth separating them at the outset.

\paragraph{(a) Chaotic amplification of an observed initial state.}
The solver is deterministic and the state is fully resolved; trajectories
diverge because a small \emph{observed} perturbation grows. Conditioned on the
state, the one-step map is a point mass, and uncertainty exists only relative
to a \emph{coarsened} conditioning set---a lead time, or a parameter with the
initial condition marginalised out. This is the weather and turbulence
setting~\cite{price2025gencast, kohl2026acdm}, and the appropriate metrics are
trajectory-level and lead-time-resolved.

\paragraph{(b) An unobservable latent field.}
The solver may again be deterministic, but a genuinely random, high-dimensional
covariate governing the response---a microstructure, a material scatter field,
an unmodelled boundary condition---is marginalised out. From the model's
viewpoint the spread is irreducible no matter how much data is collected; from
the physics viewpoint it is epistemic and would vanish if the field were
supplied. This is the classical aleatoric/epistemic
distinction~\cite{huellermeier2021aleatoric, kendall2017what}, and it is the
regime we target, because it is the one an engineering analyst actually
inhabits: the CAD model is known, the mesh is known, and the thing that decides
the answer is not in either.

\paragraph{(c) A Monte Carlo seed of a statistical process.}
Nothing varies but the random stream~\cite{plimpton2009spparks,
tran2023spparkshackathon, qin2024graingnn}. There is no condition and no
conditional; the task is distribution matching of a random field, and the
observable of interest is a set of morphological summary statistics rather than
a per-node value. This is a generative-modelling benchmark, not an
uncertainty-quantification one.

\subsection{The measurement is missing, not only the model}
\label{sec:intro:measurement}

Even a correctly specified probabilistic mesh simulator cannot currently be
\emph{shown} to be one. Mesh benchmarks in the learned-simulator
literature---MeshGraphNets' six datasets, BSMS-GNN's four, EAGLE's 1{,}184 RANS
runs, AirfRANS, DrivAerNet++'s 8{,}000 geometries, the Darcy and Navier--Stokes
operator suites---store exactly one deterministic realisation per
condition~\cite{pfaff2021meshgraphnets, janny2023eagle, elrefaie2024drivaernetpp,
li2021fourier}. Against a single draw, a collapsed predictor and a correctly
calibrated one are not merely unranked: they are formally indistinguishable,
because the RMSE-family metric being reported is minimised by the former.

Nor is swapping RMSE for CRPS sufficient. Two estimation issues intervene
before any modelling claim can be made. First, a generative simulator is
evaluated through a finite ensemble, and the naive empirical-CDF estimator of
the CRPS is biased in a direction that actively \emph{rewards} under-dispersion;
Ferro's fair scores supply the ensemble-size correction that removes this
incentive~\cite{ferro2014fair}, and Zamo and Naveau quantify the residual bias
of the competing estimators and warn specifically against comparing a
parametric predictive distribution to an ensemble using the uncorrected
form~\cite{zamo2018estimation}. The same arithmetic governs the spread--skill
diagnostic, whose $\sqrt{(M{+}1)/M}$ correction is routinely
omitted~\cite{fortin2014why}. Second, propriety is an asymptotic statement
about expectations and guarantees no statistical \emph{power} at realistic
sample sizes: the energy score is empirically the weakest discriminator of its
family and degrades with dimension~\cite{pinson2013discrimination,
alexander2024evaluating, marcotte2023regions}, which is precisely why
dependence-sensitive alternatives such as the variogram score
exist~\cite{scheuerer2015variogram}. A flat rank histogram is necessary, not
sufficient~\cite{hamill2001interpretation}, and the best-of-$N$ protocol
imported from vision is not a proper scoring rule at all---its minimiser is
approximately the square root of the true density~\cite{thiede2019analyzing}.
Finally, a single realisation does not determine a microstructural
statistic~\cite{dahari2025representativity}, so replicate counts must be
reported, not assumed.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Two mesh-native probabilistic hierarchical simulators.}
    HI-MGN-V places an MMD-InfoVAE latent at the coarse level of a per-sample
    bi-stride mesh hierarchy and learns its conditional prior by flow matching,
    jointly with the simulator. cHI-MGNflow removes the latent bottleneck and
    flow-matches the mesh increment itself, conditioned hierarchically and
    coupled by minibatch optimal transport so that few-step sampling stays
    affordable inside a several-hundred-step rollout.
  \item \textbf{A hidden-latent mesh benchmark with many realisations per
    condition}, built by \emph{withholding} the rigid-inclusion field of a
    phase-field crack-propagation dataset, together with a measured
    quantification of its residual information leak
    (\S\ref{sec:bench:construction}).
  \item \textbf{A scoring protocol that is proper by construction}:
    fair-estimator CRPS on nodal marginals and on engineering functionals, a
    variogram score and a hierarchy-matched multivariate score for spatial
    coherence, and ensemble-size-corrected spread--skill and rank-histogram
    diagnostics.
  \item \textbf{An audit of the available benchmark landscape}
    (\S\ref{sec:bench:landscape}), establishing which datasets can and cannot
    support a distributional claim, and why several widely used candidates
    cannot.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Generative PDE surrogates: stability first, distribution second}
\label{sec:related:diffusion}

Denoising diffusion entered the neural-PDE literature primarily as a stability
device rather than a distributional one. PDE-Refiner reinterprets the update
step of a neural solver as a short denoising chain, on the diagnosis that a
pointwise MSE objective systematically underweights low-amplitude spatial
frequencies, and that iterative refinement with a decreasing noise schedule
restores them~\cite{lippe2023pderefiner}. Kohl et al.\ roll a conditional
diffusion model autoregressively across an incompressible cylinder wake, a
transonic shock interaction and slices of isotropic turbulence, dominating
U-Net, FNO and VAE baselines in temporal stability and spectral
fidelity~\cite{kohl2026acdm}.

Both are generative models evaluated as deterministic ones. PDE-Refiner reports
the rollout time at which correlation with a single reference falls below a
threshold; \cite{kohl2026acdm} reports MSE, the LSiM similarity metric and
spectra over five posterior samples. Neither reports a proper scoring rule,
and---decisively---every test condition in~\cite{kohl2026acdm} is accompanied
by exactly one ground-truth trajectory, so the strongest statement the metric
can support is a distance to one draw, not a divergence between distributions.
The authors say as much. The explicitly distributional
minority~\cite{molinaro2024gencfd, rozet2023sda, du2024confild,
gao2024bayesiandiffusion, huang2024diffusionpde, shu2023physicsdiffusion,
pfister2026stocbench, srikishan2026trie, galletti2026gyroflow} proves the point
rather than refuting it; and the field's revealed preference still rewards the
mean, as in a 2026 work that deliberately removes stochastic denoising in
favour of a deterministic ODE correction and reports better accuracy and
physical consistency~\cite{dai2026flowrefiner}.

\subsection{Flow matching: why a generative prior is affordable}
\label{sec:related:fm}

Flow matching arrived as three near-simultaneous answers to one question: how
to train a continuous normalising flow without differentiating through an ODE
solver. Lipman et al.\ observed that regressing a network onto the closed-form
velocity of a \emph{conditional} probability path yields an unbiased gradient
for the intractable marginal objective~\cite{lipman2023flowmatching}; Liu et
al.\ derived the same construction from straight-line interpolations and added
rectification~\cite{liu2023rectifiedflow}; Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden obtained
the velocity as the minimiser of a quadratic loss over a stochastic
interpolant, later generalising it so that one learned drift induces a family
of deterministic and stochastic
samplers~\cite{albergo2023buildingflows, albergo2023interpolants}. The
practical consequence for us is decisive: a generative prior over simulation
states costs no more to train than a regression head, because the loss is a
mean-squared error against an analytically known target. That is what makes it
feasible to train a flow prior \emph{jointly} with a message-passing simulator
rather than as a separate second stage.

What distinguishes flow matching from diffusion, in a way that matters directly
for autoregressive physics, is that the coupling between base and target is a
free design variable. Under independent pairing the conditional velocity is an
average over all data points a given noise sample could have come from, so
trajectories are curved, the regression target is high-variance, and many
integration steps are needed. OT-CFM pairs noise and data by solving an
optimal-transport problem within each minibatch, giving in the small-noise
limit an unbiased estimator of the dynamic-OT velocity, straighter flows and
markedly lower gradient variance~\cite{tong2024otcfm}. Pooladian et al.\ prove
the property that makes this safe rather than merely convenient: any
exchangeable multisample coupling leaves the model's marginals unchanged while
reducing transport cost~\cite{pooladian2023multisample}. Conditioning can be
injected through the coupling itself~\cite{albergo2024datadependent,
chen2024follmer}. The caveat a reviewer will raise is that no work in this line
analyses \emph{accumulation} of transport error across hundreds of conditioned
autoregressive steps.

\subsection{Generative models off the grid}
\label{sec:related:offgrid}

The machinery survives the loss of a lattice, but every existing instance stops
short of the construction we need. Chen et al.\ extend flow matching to general
geometries~\cite{chen2024generalgeometries} and DeFoG to discrete graph
topology~\cite{qin2025defog}; Ramos et al.\ apply conditional flow matching to
unstructured CFD meshes without resampling~\cite{ramos2026fluidflow}; Lino et
al.\ sample equilibrium flow states directly on unstructured meshes and, in
follow-up work, factorise the sampler coarse-to-fine across mesh
levels~\cite{lino2025diffusiongraphnets, lino2026scaleautoregressive}; Li et
al.\ perform generative rollout by first mapping heterogeneous meshes onto a
unified structured latent grid~\cite{li2025genlatentfm}. Each generates
either points \emph{on} a surface, discrete topology, a flat mesh field, or a
resampled structured latent---never a continuous field factorised over a
coarsening hierarchy reconstructed per sample.

\subsection{Latent-variable surrogates and the collapse problem}
\label{sec:related:vae}

The conditional VAE posed structured prediction as sampling from $p(y \mid x)$
with a Gaussian latent~\cite{sohn2015cvae}. The factorisation is attractive for
engineering surrogates---it puts the unobserved part of the problem into a
low-dimensional code while leaving the decoder free---but it carries two
liabilities the subsequent decade has not resolved. The first is the asymmetry
between a recognition network at training time and a prior at test time, so the
sampling distribution actually used at deployment is never the one the decoder
was optimised against. The second is that under a Gaussian likelihood the
reconstruction term is a squared error, and a sufficiently powerful conditional
decoder minimises it by ignoring the latent and emitting the conditional mean.
The $\beta$-VAE made the trade-off explicit~\cite{higgins2017betavae} but
confined it to a single scalar axis; InfoVAE broke that coupling by replacing
the per-sample KL with a divergence on the \emph{aggregate} posterior, the MMD
instantiation giving an adversary-free penalty that keeps the code informative
under a flexible decoder~\cite{zhao2017infovae}. Since collapse is a property
of the likelihood rather than the bound~\cite{lucas2019dontblame}, annealing
$\beta$ treats a symptom---the training signal itself must stop rewarding the
conditional mean. A learned, past-conditioned prior is the known fix for the
train/test asymmetry~\cite{denton2018svglp, babaeizadeh2018sv2p, lee2018savp},
and it has been carried into physics surrogates and field
generation~\cite{wu2022lepde, solerarico2024betavae,
guo2022normalizingfieldflows}.

\subsection{Probabilistic weather: the one field that changed its objective}
\label{sec:related:weather}

Nowhere has the shift from deterministic surrogate to calibrated probabilistic
emulator been carried out as completely, or adjudicated as publicly. The
lineage begins with mesh graph networks almost identical in spirit to those
used for engineering simulation~\cite{keisler2022graphweather, lam2023graphcast},
with contemporaneous transformer emulators reaching the same frontier by other
routes~\cite{bi2023pangu, pathak2022fourcastnet, nguyen2024stormer,
bodnar2025aurora, kochkov2024neuralgcm}. Every one is trained on a weighted MSE
against a single reanalysis trajectory and reports RMSE and anomaly correlation
as headline skill.

That choice was quickly recognised as self-defeating: because RMSE is minimised
by the conditional mean, a model that hedges is rewarded, and as lead time
grows the optimal forecast becomes smoother than any physically realisable
state. WeatherBench~2 made this concrete, documenting explicitly that blurred
forecasts win on RMSE while failing spectral and probabilistic diagnostics, and
elevating CRPS, spread--skill, rank histograms and Brier scores to first-class
metrics~\cite{rasp2024weatherbench2}. Generative ensembles
followed~\cite{li2024seeds, price2025gencast, couairon2024archesweathergen,
zhong2024fuxiens, oskarsson2024graphefm}, and then the objective itself
changed: FuXi-ENS replaced an $L1$+KL objective with CRPS+KL precisely to stop
its decoder collapsing to a conditional mean~\cite{zhong2024fuxiens},
AIFS-CRPS trains on an ensemble-size-debiased
CRPS~\cite{lang2026aifscrps, andrae2025continuous}, and FGN attains
state-of-the-art ensemble skill from learned model perturbations trained on
per-location marginal CRPS alone~\cite{alet2025fgn, landry2025fmap}.

Two lessons transfer, and one warning. The score can be the objective. A
coarse-level latent is a working architectural answer---Graph-EFM draws a
single latent map at the coarsest level of an icosahedral hierarchy and
propagates it downward, obtaining spatially coherent members at one forward
pass each~\cite{oskarsson2024graphefm}. But the warning is that weather
verifies against a \emph{single} observed realisation per initial condition,
pooling over many cases; that works because the cases are exchangeable, and it
is exactly the assumption an engineering benchmark with a fixed geometry cannot
make.

\subsection{UQ for operators: bands, not samples}
\label{sec:related:operatoruq}

The first response of the operator-learning community was to retrofit
uncertainty onto a mean predictor. Magnani et al.\ gave the shallow neural
operator an exact GP reading and lifted it to deep operators with approximate
Bayesian deep learning~\cite{magnani2022approximatebno}; Garg and Chakraborty
replaced MCMC with variational inference for a Bayesian
DeepONet~\cite{garg2023vbdeeponet}; deep
ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017deepensembles} were carried over by Mouli
et al., who showed ensemble spread localises high-error regions under
distribution shift~\cite{mouli2024diverseno}; and Ma et al.\ supplied a
distribution-free counterpart whose functional split-conformal calibration
guarantees an expected coverage fraction~\cite{ma2024calibrated}. The survey
of Psaros et al.\ maps this space~\cite{psaros2023uqsciml}. What these share is
that they attach an \emph{interval} to a predictor that remains a conditional
mean: they quantify epistemic uncertainty that contracts with data and produce
no jointly coherent draws. The exception that proves the rule is Bülte et al.,
who obtain calibrated functional predictive distributions by replacing the
squared loss with a strictly proper scoring rule and no generative sampler at
all~\cite{bulte2025pno, cao2023residual}. Formally, the hidden-latent setting
is a posterior-predictive problem, which connects it to amortised
simulation-based inference~\cite{cranmer2020frontier, wildberger2023fmpe} and
to that literature's own calibration
crisis~\cite{hermans2022trustcrisis}.

\subsection{Microstructure: a community that already evaluates
distributionally}
\label{sec:related:micro}

Microstructure is the setting in which the inadequacy of pointwise regression
is least deniable, because the predicted object is not identified up to the
labels the loss is written in. A grain field produced by the Potts kinetic
Monte Carlo model in SPPARKS~\cite{plimpton2009spparks} is a stochastic
realisation: fixing the lattice, temperature and mobility fixes a \emph{law},
and the run-to-run difference is carried entirely by the seed. Two consequences
follow. The conditional mean of that law is not a microstructure. And grain
identifiers are arbitrary labels, so a prediction reproducing the true
morphology exactly but permuting the identifiers incurs near-maximal $L^2$
error. Any per-node, per-channel metric is measuring the wrong thing twice
over; the correct functionals are the permutation- and translation-invariant
descriptors catalogued by Torquato~\cite{torquato2002random}.

The generative-microstructure community reached this conclusion two decades ago
and has evaluated distributionally
since~\cite{mosser2017porousgan, kench2021slicegan, kench2022microlib,
seibert2022descriptor, vlassis2023diffusionmicrostructure,
lee2024anisotropicdiffusion, hoffman2025grainpaint}, while its \emph{surrogate}
half continues to regress against one seed~\cite{yan2022primme, tian2026primme3d,
qin2024graingnn, tian2025cnngnn, oommen2022microstructureoperator}. It settled,
however, on descriptor-moment agreement and KS tests, which are not proper
scores---a gap on the other side of the same problem.

\subsection{Positioning}
\label{sec:related:gap}

Four ingredients are required to make a learned mesh simulator predict a
distribution rather than a blur, and each already exists---but only in pairs.
\emph{Mesh-native generation} exists~\cite{lino2025diffusiongraphnets,
lino2026scaleautoregressive, ramos2026fluidflow}. \emph{Hierarchical
stochasticity} exists~\cite{oskarsson2024graphefm}. \emph{Proper-score training
and evaluation} exist, at operational scale~\cite{zhong2024fuxiens,
lang2026aifscrps, price2025gencast}. And \emph{multi-realisation benchmarks}
exist~\cite{molinaro2024gencfd, pfister2026stocbench}.

What does not exist is the conjunction. Every hierarchical mesh simulator is
deterministic and scored by RMSE~\cite{fortunato2022msmgn, cao2023bsmsgnn,
nabian2024xmeshgraphnet, zhdanov2025erwin}. Every generative mesh model is
either flat, steady-state, or resampled onto a structured latent grid that
discards the mesh~\cite{lino2025diffusiongraphnets, li2025genlatentfm,
ramos2026fluidflow}. Every hierarchical-latent probabilistic model lives on a
single fixed grid shared by all samples, where the hierarchy is a static
multi-resolution decomposition of one domain rather than something
reconstructed per sample from varying
geometry~\cite{oskarsson2024graphefm, price2025gencast, lam2023graphcast}.
Every proper-score-trained simulator is either
grid-bound~\cite{bulte2025pno, molinaro2024gencfd} or scores a marginal per
location~\cite{alet2025fgn, lang2026aifscrps}. And every mesh benchmark in the
learned-simulator literature stores exactly one deterministic realisation per
condition~\cite{pfaff2021meshgraphnets, janny2023eagle,
elrefaie2024drivaernetpp, li2021fourier}, so that a collapsed predictor and a
calibrated one are formally indistinguishable.

This paper closes the conjunction. HI-MGN-V places an MMD-InfoVAE latent at the
coarse level of a per-sample bi-stride mesh hierarchy and learns its
conditional prior by flow matching, jointly with the simulator; cHI-MGNflow
removes the latent bottleneck and flow-matches the mesh increment itself,
conditioned hierarchically and coupled by minibatch optimal transport. Both are
trained and reported under a protocol that is proper by
construction~\cite{gneiting2007strictly, ferro2014fair, fortin2014why,
scheuerer2015variogram, marcotte2023regions}, on a benchmark supplying many
realisations per condition with a \emph{known but hidden} source of spread.

\paragraph{Differentiation from the closest prior art.}
Lino et al.\ sample the \emph{equilibrium} distribution of a flow on a fixed
unstructured mesh, factorising the sampler coarse-to-fine in
space~\cite{lino2025diffusiongraphnets, lino2026scaleautoregressive}; we model
the \emph{conditional temporal} law of an autoregressive rollout with a
persistent latent that carries an unobservable field across steps, and score it
with strictly proper, ensemble-size-corrected rules. Graph-EFM's hierarchy is a
single fixed global grid shared by every sample, its latent is regularised by a
per-sample KL with no control over the aggregate posterior, and the uncertainty
it represents is chaotic divergence from an \emph{observed} initial
state~\cite{oskarsson2024graphefm}. GenCast's generative process is flat---a
full denoising chain at a single resolution of a fixed icosahedral mesh---and
its verification is against a single realised trajectory pooled over
cases~\cite{price2025gencast}. Li et al.\ collapse their own ensembles to an
ensemble-mean NRMSE, reporting no CRPS, energy score, spread--skill ratio or
rank histogram---a generative model scored exclusively by the point error of
its mean is being evaluated on precisely the objective its construction was
meant to escape~\cite{li2025genlatentfm}. And because a marginal score
is minimised by the correct marginal at every node and is blind to spatial
correlation~\cite{bulte2025pno, alet2025fgn}, we report both marginal and
dependence-sensitive scores, and show explicitly that the hierarchical latent
buys \emph{joint} structure a marginally-scored ensemble does not produce.
