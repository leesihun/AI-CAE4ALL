\section{The benchmark landscape for one-to-many simulation}
\label{sec:bench}

\subsection{The admission criterion}
\label{sec:bench:criterion}

A dataset can validate a distributional claim only if it supplies
\emph{multiple ground-truth realisations at a single model-visible condition}.
This is a harder requirement than it sounds, and most datasets described as
``stochastic'' in the neural-PDE literature fail it. Chaos is not sufficient: a
chaotic system with one trajectory per parameter setting still gives one draw
from the conditional, and a distance to one draw is not a divergence between
distributions. Nor is visual variability sufficient. We surveyed
$87$ candidate datasets across curated PDE suites, weather and climate
ensembles, turbulence archives, solid-mechanics and fracture sets, engineering
CAE collections, stochastic-PDE benchmarks and adjacent generative domains, and
classified each by three properties: replicate count at a fixed condition, the
representation (mesh, graph, or structured grid), and \emph{what is hidden from
the model} that creates the spread.

Table~\ref{tab:landscape} summarises the outcome. The organising finding is
negative and, we argue, publishable in itself.

Weather supplies \emph{many conditions $\times$ one truth}; large climate
initial-condition ensembles supply \emph{many truths $\times$ two conditions}.
Nobody has built many conditions $\times$ many truths on a mesh.

\subsection{What is admissible today}
\label{sec:bench:tier1}

Only a handful of obtainable datasets clear the criterion with a replicate
count adequate for a fair-CRPS estimate, and four of them belong to a single
collection~---~The Well~\cite{ohana2024well}~---~which is the only corpus that
systematically ships seed ensembles.

\paragraph{Turbulence-gravity-cooling ($100$ seeds $\times$ $27$ conditions).}
The only combination in the survey supporting \emph{both} a stable per-condition
CRPS and rank histogram \emph{and} a held-out-condition calibration test. This
is the headline conditional result. Its cost is storage
($\sim\!829$\,GB) and a free-fall-time-normalised cadence that must be
reconciled before pairing seeds.

\paragraph{Rayleigh--B\'enard ($10$ seeds $\times$ $35$ conditions).}
Chebyshev-graded wall-normal spacing makes it the most mesh-like structured set
available and mildly mis-specifies uniform-grid CNN baselines. Ten replicates
suffice for CRPS but are thin for rank histograms. At high Rayleigh and Prandtl
number the spread collapses, so per-condition spread must be reported rather
than a pooled number.

\paragraph{Turbulent radiative layer 2D ($10$ seeds $\times$ $9$ conditions).}
At $6.9$\,GB this is the ablation harness, not a result: nearly one-dimensional
conditioning makes it a strong calibration test and a weak expressivity test,
and $90$ trajectories is a small training set for a generative mesh model.

\paragraph{Viscoelastic instability (four coexisting attractors).}
The only genuinely \emph{multimodal-with-discrete-modes} dataset available, and
therefore the direct empirical answer to ``why a flow-matching prior instead of
a heteroscedastic Gaussian head'': a unimodal head provably cannot represent
four attractors. It is unconditional, so conditional calibration is untestable
here, and unequal mode counts and trajectory lengths must be reweighted
explicitly or a sampler will confuse mode weight with sampling artefact.

\paragraph{Airfoil RANS ensembles.}
The only entry whose uncertainty mechanism~---~solver non-convergence plus
turbulence-closure deficiency~---~is the one a CAE practitioner actually meets,
and the only one whose conditioning variable \emph{is a geometry}. It is
distributed regridded to a Cartesian lattice, so exercising a mesh hierarchy
requires re-running the cases or obtaining the raw mesh.

\subsection{Instructive rejections}
\label{sec:bench:reject}

Several datasets are cited in probabilistic work as though they satisfied the
criterion. They do not, and the reasons are worth naming.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One realisation per model-visible condition.} PDEBench and
    PDEArena sample the governing parameter continuously, so no two runs share
    a condition; the ACDM scenarios ship one trajectory per Reynolds or Mach
    number, which is why their ``posterior sampling'' is scored against physics
    statistics rather than a ground-truth divergence~\cite{kohl2026acdm}; JHTDB
    stores exactly one DNS per configuration; DrivAerNet++ one CFD run per
    geometry~\cite{elrefaie2024drivaernetpp}.
  \item \textbf{Temporally correlated snapshots posed as an ensemble.} Several
    archives present $N$ snapshots of \emph{one} trajectory as $N$ samples.
    That is a decorrelation-time question, not an uncertainty question, and it
    is the most common silent error in this area.
  \item \textbf{Averaged targets.} Where a benchmark releases the \emph{mean}
    of a small initial-condition ensemble as its target, an emulator wins by
    suppressing variability, and RMSE against a three-member mean is
    substantially scoring noise.
  \item \textbf{Model-form variation mistaken for aleatory spread.} Sets whose
    ``repeats'' are alternative energy decompositions, alternative constitutive
    models, or \emph{neural-network training seeds} are varying model form, not
    the physical law's randomness. Pooling them into a spread estimate is a
    category error.
\end{itemize}

The most instructive rejection is the canonical near-miss. Mechanical MNIST
Crack Path exhibits dramatic path sensitivity under an identical loading
protocol, and is regularly invoked as evidence of stochastic behaviour. But as
published, the realised inclusion centres are handed to the network as a
$64\times64$ rigidity array and the resulting map is solved deterministically at
$\mathrm{F}_1 = 0.870$~\cite{mohammadzadeh2022crackpath}. \emph{Sensitivity is
not uncertainty.} Three separate recent fracture and damage benchmarks shipped
with visually dramatic stochastic-looking behaviour and zero aleatory
replicates. Naming this confusion, we think, is overdue.

\subsection{Our construction, and its measured information floor}
\label{sec:bench:construction}

We turn the canonical near-miss into an admissible benchmark by an explicit,
declared act of information withholding. The rigid-inclusion field is
\emph{not} converted; the model sees reference coordinates, the damage and
displacement state, and nothing else. Every case then presents a bit-identical
visible input, so the released samples are draws from a single conditional and
the empirical conditional distribution is directly observable.

We report four properties measured on the converted data, because a
withholding construction is only defensible if its consequences are stated
rather than assumed.

\paragraph{The spread is large and continuous.}
Pairwise relative $L^2$ of the damage field rises from $0.047$ at the first
stored step to $0.948$ at the last; final-crack IoU averages $0.283$ with a
$95$th percentile of $0.473$. A PCA of the final damage fields requires $43$
components for $90\%$ of variance, and no sample has a nearest-neighbour crack
IoU above $0.9$. There is no discrete mode structure: the outcome distribution
is continuous and high-dimensional, which is precisely why a
descriptor-histogram acceptance test would be inadequate and a proper score is
required.

\paragraph{The latent is structured, not i.i.d.}
Inclusion centres are seeded on bitmap pixels above an intensity threshold,
with sub-pixel jitter and a minimum-spacing rejection rule. The hidden variable
is therefore a structured, class-clustered prior with a genuinely random
realisation drawn on top of it~---~not white noise. Any paper using this
construction should say so; the distinction changes what a well-calibrated
prior is expected to look like.

\paragraph{The withholding is not perfect, and we quantify the leak.}
The first stored frame is not the pristine notch but an early loading step, so
the recorded displacements already encode the \emph{elastic response} to the
hidden inclusions. Nearest-neighbour retrieval on that first frame predicts the
final crack at IoU $0.2726$ against a random baseline of $0.1833$
($z = +24.7$; Spearman $\rho = +0.240$, reproducing held-out and on the test
split, and surviving a crack-size control at partial $\rho = +0.199$). The
latent partially images itself into the visible state. The practical size of
the leak is small~---~a best guess moves from $0.18$ to $0.27$ against a mean
pairwise IoU of $0.283$~---~so the conditional remains broad, but we report
this as a measured \emph{floor} on achievable sharpness rather than claiming a
clean hidden-variable setup.

\paragraph{The branching window is narrow.}
The stochasticity is concentrated in roughly three of nineteen stored steps.
The earliest steps are frozen (per-step change below the cross-sample spread,
so an autoregressive model wins by copying), a short window branches sharply
(nearest neighbours diverge by factors of $3.1$, $9.1$ and $4.9$ over
consecutive steps), and thereafter the outcome is locked (top-$1$
nearest-neighbour retention returns to $40/40$). A one-step score averaged over
the trajectory therefore dilutes the real signal roughly six-fold, and must be
reported step-resolved.

\subsection{Two negative results on candidate slots}
\label{sec:bench:negative}

We record two datasets we prepared and then rejected, because both failures are
general.

\paragraph{A chaotic ensemble is not an aleatory one.}
A radiative-mixing-layer ensemble with nine cooling-time values and ten seeds
appears ideal. It is not, for the task an autoregressive simulator actually
trains: the seeds differ \emph{only} in an initial velocity perturbation, and
density and pressure at the first step are bitwise identical across seeds. That
perturbation is stored in the state rows the model reads as input. The seed is
therefore fully observed, and $p(u_{t+1} \mid u_t, c)$ is a point mass for a
deterministic solver on a fully resolved state. Genuine spread exists only for
the trajectory-given-condition map, where cross-seed spread grows to exceed the
cross-condition spread by the final step. The dataset is a legitimate
\emph{chaotic ensemble-spread} benchmark; it is not an aleatory-uncertainty
benchmark, and one-step CRPS on it degenerates toward mean absolute error. Two
replicates per condition in a standard $80/10/10$ split~---~a $75.6\%$ relative
standard error on the spread estimate and a three-bin rank histogram~---~would
in any case not support a calibration claim.

\paragraph{An unconditional generative task is not a UQ benchmark, and grain
labels are not a field.}
A Potts kinetic Monte Carlo grain-growth set with one thousand seeds is a
textbook stochastic process, but it carries no conditioning variable at all:
every sample presents an identical input, so $p(y \mid x) \equiv p(y)$. Worse,
the target is a labelling, not a field. Relabelling a sample by a permutation
that provably preserves the partition (boundary-indicator IoU exactly $1.0$)
scores a mean squared error statistically indistinguishable from comparing two
\emph{genuinely different} microstructures, while a constant field scores twice
as well as either. Under a pointwise loss, mode collapse is the global optimum.
This survives the obvious repair: even on a permutation-invariant $0/1$
grain-boundary target, a spatially constant predictor attains a Brier score of
$0.2469$ against the true ensemble's $0.2624$~---~the constant \emph{beats} the
truth by $5.9\%$~---~and a spatially scrambled ensemble ties it, because the
per-node marginal standard deviation ($0.1568$) sits at the independent-Bernoulli
null ($0.1572$). Per-node scoring here is not weak but anti-informative. The
generative-microstructure community's choice of morphological-distribution
metrics~\cite{torquato2002random, hoffman2025grainpaint} is correct, and a
pointwise proper score is simply the wrong instrument.

\subsection{The benchmark that should exist}
\label{sec:bench:missing}

We state the design requirements explicitly, each traceable to a failure above.
A one-to-many CAE benchmark needs: (1)~at least $50$ members at each of at
least $50$ conditions, with fair-CRPS reported under the finite-ensemble
correction~\cite{ferro2014fair}; (2)~a native mesh with connectivity retained
and nodal fields, since every dataset that regridded destroyed exactly the
structure a hierarchical mesh network exists to exploit; (3)~a \emph{labelled
uncertainty-source axis}, because no CAE benchmark today distinguishes ``my
solver with a different seed'' from ``a different solver''; (4)~a paired
deterministic control on which the predicted spread must collapse, without
which a model that hallucinates spread is indistinguishable from a calibrated
one; and (5)~an engineering quantity-of-interest distribution, since
calibration on a stress field that is miscalibrated on peak stress is
worthless.
